**Min’s Speed-Dating Interview #2: Student Perspectives on Professor Recognition Glasses**

**Participant background:** UIUC junior in Information Sciences; lectures with 150 to 200 students and seminars with 20 to 30  
**Format:** 20-minute interview using three storyboard frames

**Background and lived experience**

**Min:** Do your professors usually know your name?  
**Participant:** “In smaller classes, usually. In big lectures, probably only if I regularly attend office hours.”

**Min:** How did you feel when a professor remembered your name?  
**Participant:** “A seminar professor remembered my name and a topic I’d mentioned. It made me feel like they actually noticed my contribution.”

**Min:** What about when a professor got it wrong?  
**Participant:** “One professor kept using my legal name instead of my preferred name. Correcting them in front of everyone felt awkward.”

**Min:** Has that affected whether you participated or asked for help?  
**Participant:** “I spoke up more in the seminar. In large lectures, I usually waited until afterward to ask questions because I felt less comfortable.”

**Min:** How do professors handle your preferred name or pronunciation?  
**Participant:** “I usually email them at the beginning of the semester. Some remember, but others keep using whatever is on the roster.”

**Frame 1: The remembered name**

**Min:** What’s your first reaction to glasses showing your name and pronunciation?  
**Participant:** “That could help, but I’d immediately wonder what else the glasses can see.”

**Min:** When would it feel helpful or uncomfortable?  
**Participant:** “Helpful during office hours or when I approach them after class. Uncomfortable if it makes it easier to unexpectedly call on me in a huge lecture.”

**Frame 2: The wrong name**

**Min:** What information would you be comfortable displaying?  
**Participant:** “My preferred name, pronunciation, and pronouns if I choose to share them. Previous questions only if I specifically agreed to include them.”

**Min:** What would cross the line?  
**Participant:** “Grades appearing over my face, attendance tracking, location, social media, or guesses about my personality or ability.”

**Min:** What should happen if the system gets your name or pronouns wrong?  
**Participant:** “A brief apology, then use the correct information. Let me fix it easily without turning it into a discussion in front of the class.”

**Frame 3: Choice and consent**

**Min:** Would you opt in?  
**Participant:** “Maybe, if I can choose each field, no footage is stored, and I can turn it off or delete my information whenever I want.”

**Min:** Would you feel comfortable saying no?  
**Participant:** “Not completely. The professor grades me, so I might worry they’d think I was being difficult. Opting out should be private and have no effect on how I’m treated.”

**Optional follow-ups**

**Min:** Who should control the data, and how long should it stay?  
**Participant:** “Students should control their information. The university could manage the system, with access limited to selected professors and deletion when the course ends.”

**Min:** What if the camera sees students who haven’t opted in?  
**Participant:** “That still worries me. They shouldn’t be identified or recorded just because they’re sitting nearby.”

**Min:** Does the setting change your comfort level?  
**Participant:** “It seems most useful in large classes. In a small seminar, I’d expect the professor to learn our names. Hallway recognition would feel more intrusive.”

**Min:** What would build or break your trust?  
**Participant:** “A clear demonstration of what appears and simple privacy controls would help. Hidden tracking or unexpected new features would make me refuse.”

**Min:** Would you worry about future attendance or grading uses?  
**Participant:** “Yes. I’d want a clear rule against using it for attendance or grading, and fresh consent before the purpose changes.”

**Wrap-up**

**Min:** If you could change one thing, what would it be?  
**Participant:** “Let students choose exactly what appears and which professors can see it.”

**Key takeaways**

- **Value:** Name and pronunciation support could help students feel recognized.
- **Accuracy:** Corrections should be quick, discreet, and controlled by students.
- **Consent:** Students may feel pressured because professors evaluate them.
- **Boundaries:** Limit displayed information and prevent expansion into tracking or grading.
