
## Interviewee 7 — Senior Mechanical Engineering Student

**Profile:** Fourth-year mechanical engineering student; classes of approximately 25–120 students. Generally likes technology but prioritizes accuracy and efficiency.

### Interview Questions and Responses

**Q1. How big are your classes? Do your professors usually know your name?**

My classes range from about 25 to 120 students. Professors in smaller classes usually know my name, but professors in large lectures rarely do.

**Q2. Can you tell me about a time when a professor knew your name? How did that make you feel?**

My professor remembered my name during a design project presentation. It made me feel like they actually noticed the work I was putting into the class.

**Q3. Can you tell me about a time when a professor got your name wrong or didn't know your name? How did you feel?**

One professor mixed up my name with another student during a lab demonstration. It was awkward for a moment, but I didn't take it personally.

**Q4. Has a professor knowing or not knowing your name ever affected whether you asked questions, went to office hours, or asked for help? Can you give an example?**

Not really. I go to office hours when I need help regardless, but it definitely makes conversations easier when professors remember me.

**Q5. Do you use a preferred name or pronouns, or do people often mispronounce your name? How do professors usually handle that?**

I use my first name, and people usually pronounce it correctly. I haven't experienced many issues with that.

**Q6. Imagine a professor wearing smart glasses that quietly show a student's name and pronunciation when they look at them. What is your first reaction?**

I think it's a smart idea, but I'd be curious how accurately the glasses work. If they make mistakes frequently, they might create more problems than they solve.

**Q7. When would this feel helpful to you? When would it feel uncomfortable?**

It would be helpful during project meetings and office hours. It would feel uncomfortable if the professor kept looking at a screen instead of actually talking to me.

**Q8. What information would you be comfortable with the professor seeing, such as your name, pronunciation, pronouns, notes you provided, or previous questions?**

My name, major, current class project, and maybe previous academic questions I've asked.

**Q9. What information would definitely cross the line for you, such as grades, attendance, location, photos, social media, or inferred information?**

My location, personal messages, grades in other classes, and anything unrelated to my academic work.

**Q10. If the system showed the wrong name or misgendered you, what do you think should happen?**

The professor should be able to correct it immediately. The glasses shouldn't keep showing information if they aren't confident it's accurate.

**Q11. Would you opt in to use a system like this? What would need to be true for you to say yes?**

Yes, but only if the technology was reliable and I could choose what information professors see.

**Q12. Would you feel comfortable saying no to your professor? Would you worry that opting out might affect how they see you? Why or why not?**

Yes, but I think students should be able to opt out privately rather than having to explain themselves to the professor.

**Q13. Who should control the data, and how long should it be stored?**

Students should control their own profiles. I think the data should be deleted when the course ends unless the student chooses to keep it.

**Q14. If the camera also sees students who have not opted in, how would you feel about that? What would make it acceptable?**

I wouldn't mind if it briefly detected people without saving their images, but it shouldn't identify or track anyone who hasn't agreed.

**Q15. Would your comfort level change depending on whether it was used in a large lecture, small seminar, office hours, or hallway?**

Yes. I'd be fine with it during office hours or project meetings, but it seems unnecessary in hallways.

**Q16. What would make you trust this system? What would make you refuse to use it?**

Seeing accuracy testing results and knowing exactly how my information is stored and protected.

**Q17. Would you worry that this technology could eventually be used for attendance tracking or grading? What guarantees would you want?**

Yes. I'd want a guarantee that the system isn't connected to attendance records or grading software.

**Q18. How would you feel if the glasses recognized you correctly sometimes but made mistakes in different classroom settings, such as large lectures or small seminars?**

That would make me trust the system less. If it only works under perfect conditions, professors might be better off asking students for their names.

**Q19. How would you feel if a professor had to pause for a few seconds while the glasses identified you or displayed your information?**

That would feel awkward, especially during a conversation. The information needs to appear almost instantly or the professor should just ask.

**Q20. Do you think the benefits of these glasses would outweigh the cost, battery limitations, and effort required to use them? Why or why not?**

Maybe, but it depends on how expensive the glasses are and how well they work. I wouldn't think it's worth buying expensive hardware just to remember names unless it noticeably improves interactions.

**Q21. Have you used AI tools, such as ChatGPT or other technology, that gave you incorrect information, responded too slowly, or made a task harder? How would those experiences affect your trust in these smart glasses?**

Yes, I've used ChatGPT to help understand engineering concepts, and sometimes it gives incorrect calculations. I usually double-check the answers. I'd want professors to verify information from these glasses instead of trusting it automatically.
