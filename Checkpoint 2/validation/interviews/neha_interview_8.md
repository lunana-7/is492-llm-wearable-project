## Interviewee 6 — First-Year Psychology Student

**Profile:** First-year psychology student; introductory lectures of approximately 150–250 students and discussion sections of 18–25 students. Values personal interactions and is cautious about AI in education.

### Interview Questions and Responses

**Q1. How big are your classes? Do your professors usually know your name?**

Most of my lectures have around 150 to 250 students, so professors usually don't know my name. My discussion sections are smaller, and the TAs recognize me more often.

**Q2. Can you tell me about a time when a professor knew your name? How did that make you feel?**

My discussion instructor remembered my name after I participated in class. It made me feel more comfortable speaking up because I felt like I wasn't just another student.

**Q3. Can you tell me about a time when a professor got your name wrong or didn't know your name? How did you feel?**

I introduced myself to a professor during office hours, but they didn't remember me the next time. I wasn't upset, but it made the interaction feel less personal.

**Q4. Has a professor knowing or not knowing your name ever affected whether you asked questions, went to office hours, or asked for help? Can you give an example?**

Yes. I feel more comfortable asking questions when the instructor recognizes me. In large lectures, I sometimes avoid asking questions because I feel anonymous.

**Q5. Do you use a preferred name or pronouns, or do people often mispronounce your name? How do professors usually handle that?**

I don't use a different preferred name, but I would want the option to choose what information professors see about me.

**Q6. Imagine a professor wearing smart glasses that quietly show a student's name and pronunciation when they look at them. What is your first reaction?**

I think it's interesting, but I also think it's a little strange that professors would need facial recognition technology to remember students.

**Q7. When would this feel helpful to you? When would it feel uncomfortable?**

It could be helpful in smaller discussion sections where professors interact with students frequently. It would be uncomfortable if I felt like I was constantly being watched.

**Q8. What information would you be comfortable with the professor seeing, such as your name, pronunciation, pronouns, notes you provided, or previous questions?**

Probably just my name, pronunciation, and pronouns if I choose to share them.

**Q9. What information would definitely cross the line for you, such as grades, attendance, location, photos, social media, or inferred information?**

My grades, attendance, location, social media, and any assumptions the AI makes about my personality or behavior.

**Q10. If the system showed the wrong name or misgendered you, what do you think should happen?**

The professor should apologize and ask for the correct information. I should also be able to update my profile so it doesn't happen again.

**Q11. Would you opt in to use a system like this? What would need to be true for you to say yes?**

I'm not sure. I'd want to see how it works and understand exactly what happens to my information before deciding.

**Q12. Would you feel comfortable saying no to your professor? Would you worry that opting out might affect how they see you? Why or why not?**

I might feel uncomfortable if the professor personally asked me to participate. I would rather have an anonymous or private way to opt out.

**Q13. Who should control the data, and how long should it be stored?**

Students should have full control. I think everything should be deleted at the end of the semester, and students should be able to delete their information earlier.

**Q14. If the camera also sees students who have not opted in, how would you feel about that? What would make it acceptable?**

That would concern me. I wouldn't want my face analyzed without permission, even if the system didn't save the image.

**Q15. Would your comfort level change depending on whether it was used in a large lecture, small seminar, office hours, or hallway?**

Yes. I would be more comfortable with it during office hours than in a large lecture or hallway. It should only be used when it's actually necessary.

**Q16. What would make you trust this system? What would make you refuse to use it?**

Clear privacy policies, student consent, and knowing that the professor isn't secretly recording or tracking students.

**Q17. Would you worry that this technology could eventually be used for attendance tracking or grading? What guarantees would you want?**

Definitely. I wouldn't want professors using the glasses to monitor who comes to class or how often students participate.

**Q18. How would you feel if the glasses recognized you correctly sometimes but made mistakes in different classroom settings, such as large lectures or small seminars?**

I would probably stop trusting them. If professors call students by the wrong names because of the glasses, it could make people feel worse instead of more recognized.

**Q19. How would you feel if a professor had to pause for a few seconds while the glasses identified you or displayed your information?**

It would make the conversation feel unnatural. I'd rather have the professor ask for my name than wait awkwardly for technology.

**Q20. Do you think the benefits of these glasses would outweigh the cost, battery limitations, and effort required to use them? Why or why not?**

I'm not convinced. Remembering names is helpful, but I think professors could also use seating charts or make more effort to learn students' names without expensive technology.

**Q21. Have you used AI tools, such as ChatGPT or other technology, that gave you incorrect information, responded too slowly, or made a task harder? How would those experiences affect your trust in these smart glasses?**

Yes. I've used AI study tools that gave explanations that sounded correct but weren't accurate. That makes me cautious about trusting AI with personal information, especially if it could identify the wrong person.
