# Min's Speed-Dating Interview #2 — Student (professor-facing glasses)

**Who:** A UIUC student (any major/year)
**Format:** ~15–20 minutes, speed-dating style — core questions first, marked optionals if time runs short
**Goal:** Understand how students feel about professors using recognition glasses: helpful memory aid or surveillance?

---

## Storyboard frames (show on your phone/laptop)

**Frame 1 — The remembered name.** *A professor wearing smart glasses looks at a student in a 200-person lecture. The overlay quietly shows: "Min Kim — she/her — 'min' (rhymes with 'win')". The professor greets them by name.*
**Frame 2 — The wrong name.** *The overlay shows the wrong name. The professor calls on "Sarah" — the student's name is Min. The lecture hall goes quiet.*
**Frame 3 — The choice.** *A phone screen: "Opt in to name display for your professors? You can change this anytime." Two buttons: Opt in / Not now.*

---

## The script

**0:00–2:00 — Warm-up (core)**
> "I'm researching an idea for class: professors wearing smart glasses that quietly show a student's name and pronunciation when they look at them. I want your honest reactions — especially what would bother you. No recording, just notes, no real names."
1. "How big are your classes? Do your professors usually know your name?"

**2:00–5:00 — Lived experience (core)**
2. "Can you tell me about a time when a professor knew your name? How did that make you feel?"
3. "Can you tell me about a time when a professor got your name wrong or didn't know it? How did you feel?"
4. "Has a professor knowing — or not knowing — your name ever affected whether you asked questions, went to office hours, or asked for help? Any example?"

**5:00–7:00 — Identity (core)**
5. "Do you use a preferred name or pronouns, or do people often mispronounce your name? How do professors usually handle that?"

**7:00–10:00 — Concept reaction (core)** *(show Frame 1)*
6. "Imagine a professor wearing smart glasses that quietly show your name and pronunciation when they look at you. What's your first reaction?"
7. "When would this feel helpful to you? When would it feel uncomfortable?"

**10:00–13:00 — Boundaries (core)** *(show Frame 2)*
8. "What info would you be comfortable with the professor seeing — name, pronunciation, pronouns, notes you provided, previous questions?"
9. "What would definitely cross the line — grades, attendance, location, photos, social media, inferred info?"
10. "If the system showed the wrong name or misgendered you, what should happen?"

**13:00–16:00 — Power & consent (core)** *(show Frame 3)*
11. "Would you opt in? What would need to be true for you to say yes?"
12. "Would you feel comfortable saying no to your professor? Would you worry opting out affects how they see you? Why?"

**Optionals (if time / strong rapport)**
13. "Who should control the data, and how long should it be stored?"
14. "If the camera also sees students who haven't opted in, how would you feel? What would make that acceptable?"
15. "Would your comfort change between a large lecture, small seminar, office hours, or hallway?"
16. "What would make you trust this system? What would make you refuse it?"
17. "Would you worry this could eventually be used for attendance tracking or grading? What guarantees would you want?"

**Wrap**
- "If you could change one thing about this idea, what would it be?"
